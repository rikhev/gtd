# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Delegated: a TypeScript web app served locally. React + Vite on the front end, a small Node server (Hono) for file parsing, calendar feeds and the login, and SQLite for storage. Chosen because it keeps everything on one machine, keeps calendar links and secrets off the client, and makes dense editable lists straightforward. It runs in the browser at localhost; a desktop wrapper can come later without a rewrite.

## Users

One person: the owner, on their own Mac. They practice (or want to practice) David Allen's Getting Things Done and want the capture → clarify → organize loop to take less effort. The expected power use is keyboard-heavy and list-centric, in the spirit of the classic Outlook Tasks view.

## Product Purpose

A personal GTD system where capture has no friction and clarification is quick. The owner dumps "stuff" into an inbox by typing or uploading documents, then clarifies it one item at a time, deciding for each whether it is actionable and what it becomes: projects, next actions, waiting-for, someday/maybe, reference, or trash. The result lives in compact, directly editable lists.

Success means the inbox reaches zero regularly and every active project has a next action. The owner trusts the lists enough to work from them.

## Positioning

Clarifying is the GTD step people skip because it takes thought. This product makes that thinking fast: one item at a time, GTD's questions asked in order, every decision a keystroke. Nothing moves out of the inbox without the owner's say-so. There are no AI features (owner's decision): the owner does the thinking, and the app keeps it quick.

## Operating Context

- Runs locally in a browser tab (no login on localhost), or self-hosted on the owner's VPS at gtd.rikard.me behind HTTPS with a single-owner login: password + TOTP authenticator code, one-time recovery codes, 30-day HttpOnly sessions, and login rate limiting. There is one user and no sync between instances. Deployment is documented in DEPLOY.md.
- Capture comes from free typing (quick-add) and from uploaded documents. The accepted document types are still undecided.
- Includes a recurring Weekly Review ritual, with its checks built in.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- GTD lists at launch: Inbox, Projects, Next Actions, Contexts (tags on next actions, e.g. @computer, @phone), Waiting For, Someday/Maybe, Reference, Checklists, Weekly Review.
- The clarify step is the owner's decision, one item at a time.
- Project and next-action lists are compact, inline-editable tables/lists (the Outlook Tasks model).
- Next Actions are grouped by context by default (where the owner is and what tools are at hand); grouping by project is also offered (owner's request).
- Waiting For is grouped by who or what it waits on (Group by waiting on), never by project; the Waiting on column is hidden while grouped by it. A new item added inside a group offers that name first. The names offered when asking "waiting on" are the ones on items in Waiting For right now; an item moved back to Next or Someday drops its waiting on, since and follow-up (⌘Z brings them back).
- Next Actions and Waiting For can also be grouped by importance (owner's decision: "important", not "flagged for today"): an Important group first, then Everything else. Marking an action important (Ins, ⌘I, or a click on its marker) shows a red exclamation mark, as Outlook marks high importance, and makes its subject bold; dropping a row into the Important group marks it.
- **Keyboard-only is a hard requirement.** The whole interface must work without a mouse. Every view, list, row, field, action, dialog, and Clarify decision needs a keyboard shortcut or a keyboard path. The mouse is optional and never required.
- Shortcuts follow **Outlook on the web** conventions, not classic desktop Outlook. On the Mac, Ctrl becomes ⌘ everywhere (owner's rule). The one exception is the go-to keys: Control on every system, ⌃ on the Mac too (owner's decision), in rail order counted per group so every key sits under one hand: ⌃1–6 for the first group (Inbox, Calendar, Next Actions, Waiting For, Agendas, Projects) and ⌃⇧1–5 for the second group and the Trash (Someday, Reference, Checklists, Done, Trash; owner's request: ⌃7–9 needed a second hand), because the browser owns ⌘1–8 (tabs), macOS owns ⌘⇧3–5 (screenshots) and ⌘⌥ is too awkward to hold. On Windows and Linux the page takes Ctrl+1–6 over the browser's tab switching; Ctrl+Shift+digits are free. Control with a letter is never used for them: on the Mac those edit text (⌃A, ⌃E), elsewhere the browser owns many (Ctrl+W, Ctrl+R). Mac aliases cover missing keys (Insert, Home/End). Browser- and OS-reserved keys are never used. GTD-only actions get extra single-letter keys in the same style. Capture is in-app only for now; a global hotkey is deferred. There are no confirmation dialogs; every action is undoable with ⌘Z, announced in a brief toast (there is no status bar). Shortcuts are discovered through a ⌘K command palette (plus Outlook's ⇧? overlay), not printed on the lists. The exceptions are the rail, where every stop shows its shortcut as a key cap, and the letter-key modes, Clarify and the Weekly Review: each carries one quiet line naming its few keys (owner's decisions after the first and fourth critiques). The palette lists recently used commands first, then the current screen's own commands, then navigation, then cursor movement. Key legends show modifier symbols on every system (⌃ Control, ⇧ Shift; ⌘ and ⌥ on the Mac, ◇ for Alt elsewhere), never "Ctrl+Shift+" words (owner's decision).
- Target browsers: Firefox and Microsoft Edge (Chromium) on macOS. Both must be tested, including every shortcut.
- Data is local-first in SQLite.
- Terminology follows GTD: stuff, capture, clarify, organize, reflect, engage, next action, project, context, waiting for, someday/maybe, reference.
- Naming: next actions and projects are both written verb-first ("Call Anna about the Q3 figures", "Update the user guide for the new software"). The project title is the expected outcome; there is no separate outcome field.
- **Capture inputs:** typed text, pasted text or email, and uploaded PDF, DOCX, TXT/MD, images, .eml and .msg. A document enters as one inbox item; clarifying may turn it into several outcomes, and the source file stays linked.
- **Done items:** leave the active lists but stay on their project's timeline and in a searchable, dated Done log. The Weekly Review uses this log.
- **Project timeline** (owner's request): a project's detail pane lists every action it has had, in the order they were created (oldest first, the newest just above "Add a next action"): next actions, Waiting For items and someday ones, and done ones struck through, including those archived to Done. Deleted actions don't show. Each row ends with its date: when it was done, or when it was added; the heading counts the open ones and notes how many are done.
- **Recurring actions:** the next occurrence is created when the current one is marked done, with its dates moved forward. Recurrence is set as natural text ("every 3 months", "every mon").
- **Waiting For:** every item must name who or what it is waiting on. Every way into Waiting For asks for it first (moving, delegating, creating directly, filing from the Inbox, Clarify), and cancelling changes nothing. Items also record since when (set automatically), a follow-up date that resurfaces the item as something to chase, and an optional project link. A waiting item counts as its project's next step, so the project isn't flagged as stalled.
- **Tickler:** any item can get a "bring back on" date, and on that date it returns to the Inbox. Defer (start) dates hide next actions until their date.
- **Areas of focus:** projects belong to areas (#Work, #Home, #Health…; shown with a # as contexts are with an @, owner's decision), which group the Projects list and the review. There is no Areas page (owner's decision: grouping Projects by area already shows it); areas are added from any area picker and renamed, reordered or deleted in Settings › Areas. Projects can be narrowed to areas (owner's request): F on Projects asks "Which areas?" (tick one or more, No area included). Each tick applies at once and the picker comes back on the row just ticked, so several can be ticked in a row; Done closes it, Every area clears it (owner's bug report: ticks used to wait for a Show row, and Esc or a click away kept nothing). It stays until cleared (a way of looking, not a moment, unlike What fits now). The heading's count follows it ("4 of 12 active projects"), a line above the list names the areas with Change and Show all, the View menu (⌥V) offers it too, and "Filter by area" sits in the heading when it is off. A new project made while narrowed takes the first chosen area, so it stays in sight.
- **Project support:** each project has a notes page and attached files.
- **Weekly Review:** a guided checklist that runs mind sweep → get clear → projects → next actions → Waiting For → Someday/Maybe → look back → upcoming → checklists → get creative (owner's decision, after a GTD gap review: the sweep and the look back were missing). Built-in checks flag stalled projects, stale waiting items and overdue actions. The date of the last review is shown.
- **Export:** everything can be exported as Markdown (one file per list) plus a JSON dump, so there is no lock-in. Automatic backup and import are not in scope for now.
- **Theme:** follows the macOS appearance, with a light daytime theme and a dark evening theme, both designed in their own right.
- **Not in scope yet:** global capture hotkey, multiple devices, import.
- No clarification writes "Captured: …" into an action's notes (owner's decision). Clarifying carries the item's own notes (everything under its first line) over to the first action as they are; a one-line capture leaves the notes empty.
- **Calendar** (owner's request): GTD's hard landscape, second in the rail right after the Inbox (⌃2), because it is checked before the lists are worked. Week, month and year views of the dates the system holds: an action's start (defer) and due, a project's start and due (projects gained a start date for it), Waiting For follow-ups and Someday ticklers. Start and due draw one bar across the days between; either end drags to change that date, the bar drags to move both. Done and deleted work stays off it. Weeks start on Monday, or Sunday in Settings › Calendar.
- On Projects and in the Weekly Review's Projects step, W adds a Waiting For item straight to the project under the cursor (what, then who or what; waiting since today), as T adds a next action (owner's request: the two are a pair, both single letters, in a project's detail pane too; the Weekly Review, once a week, moved to ⇧R); a waiting item counts as the project's next step.
- A Waiting For item can be added from anywhere with ⌥W (owner's request): what you're waiting for, who or what you wait on (required, as everywhere in Waiting For; recent names are offered), then its project if any. It is waiting since today; a follow-up date is set on the item.
- A next action can be added from anywhere with ⌥T (owner's request): what to do, its context (required, as everywhere), then its project if it has one ("No project" first; a new name creates the project). It lands on Next Actions and the view stays where it was.
- A project can be started from anywhere with ⌥N (owner's request): its outcome (verb first), its area or none, then its first next action with a context, as GTD asks of every project; Esc at that step keeps the project without one. On the Projects list N still adds one in place.
- When clarifying by hand, an item can be made a project directly (⇧P, or File as › Whole item → New project; owner's request): the project takes the item's name, the proposed actions go into it, and the first action is ready to be named as the next step.
- A next action that turns out to need more than one step can be turned into a project (⇧P, the Move picker, ⌘K; owner's request): the action becomes the project's outcome and carries its notes, files, start/due dates, tickler and area (from the project it was in, whose name is noted); the first next action is asked for at once, keeping the old action's context.
- Deleted items are kept for a week by default in the Trash (⌃⇧5), adjustable in Settings (1–365 days), and can be restored where they were until then; after that the server removes them and their files (owner's request).
- Done works as in classic Outlook (owner's decision): a done action stays on its list, greyed and struck through at the bottom of its group, until archived; each action list can show or hide its done actions and archive them to Done: ⇧E archives every done item on every list at once, actions and completed projects alike (owner's request), and a list's View menu archives that list alone. Done holds everything archived, actions and completed projects alike (owner's request: like the Trash, it shows everything), grouped by the day it was done or by project; E puts an item back (a project with the actions closed along with it) and Delete trashes it. Existing done actions were treated as already archived.
- Each view has its own address (#inbox, #projects, #reference…), so the browser's Back and Forward move between views and a reload or bookmark lands on the same list (owner's request). Clarify has an entry (Back leaves it) but can't be reopened from an address, so it lands on the Inbox; search is a query, not a place, and adds no entry. The tab title names the view ("Projects · Stiltje").

## Brand Commitments

Colour appears only where it makes a difference: blue for focus and selection, red for trouble, the traffic-light lamps. There is no yellow in the interface.

## Product Principles

1. **Capture must be instant.** Getting something into the inbox should never require deciding anything.
2. **The owner decides.** Nothing is filed, changed or learned without an explicit decision; there are no AI features.
3. **Density over decoration.** The lists are working surfaces: many rows visible and editable in place.
4. **Keyboard first, everything has a shortcut.** If an action can't be done from the keyboard, it isn't finished.
5. **Faithful to GTD.** Use the method's vocabulary and flow rather than inventing a new productivity model.
6. **The owner's data stays with the owner.** It is local-first and portable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Full keyboard operability is a product requirement, not just an accessibility nicety. Focus must always be visible, and nothing can trap it. Every command must be discoverable from the keyboard (a shortcut reference and command palette). Semantic markup (grid/listbox roles) should keep the lists usable with a screen reader: the active list holds real keyboard focus and points `aria-activedescendant` at the cursor row, "selected" means ticked rows only (a plain move or click clears them, as in Finder and Explorer; ⇧ extends a range, ⌘/Ctrl-click ticks one, and a drag draws a selection rectangle, adding to the selection with ⌘/Ctrl), and every view change lands focus on the new list.
- The Weekly Review's progress survives closing the tab (a review untouched for a week starts over). Finish leads with what was cleared ("4 of 6 steps clear · 3 items clarified"), then lists what is still open. It never claims more than is true: the tape reads "Ready to record" while steps are open, and the recorded message only says "Your system is current" when none are (otherwise it says how many were left open). Get clear lists the Inbox as a list like the other steps (kind, subject, files, captured); V files the focused or ticked items, E marks them done (two-minute rule) and Delete trashes them, all without leaving the review. K runs Clarify inside the step, under the review's step bar; Esc returns to the list (owner's decision: clarifying and filing never take the owner out of the review). The Projects step shows each project's next action, or who it waits on. The rail says "not yet" before a first review and only turns red once a review is more than a week old.
- Toasts name the item when there is one ("“Pay the VAT for Q3” done"). ⌘Z returns the cursor to the restored row. A command on a group heading says so instead of doing nothing, and Esc from the capture bar says the draft is kept.
- Filing (V, "File", in the Inbox, or from an Inbox item's detail pane) covers the whole clarify decision in one picker, in GTD order: a list; done now (two-minute rule) or trash; an existing project; or a new project (type its name, as the placeholder says; the item becomes its first action). Move and File as list destinations in the same order with the same names.
- The rail is a status panel: the Inbox (an ordinary rail item with its count), the lists in order of use with counts only where they ask for something (overdue, to chase, stalled, done today), and a system check (review age, oldest Inbox item; stalled projects and follow-ups due show on the Projects and Waiting For stops instead) whose rows open where each is fixed. On phones it becomes a bottom tab bar (Inbox, Next, Waiting, More).
- The detail pane opens with focus on the pane, not in a field. It is walked like every other region (owner's decision): a cursor sits on one field at a time, starting on the item's title; ↑↓ move it through the fields, the timeline's rows and the add line; Enter opens the field's picker or edits it. Each field's key still jumps straight to it and opens it, taking the cursor along (the pane prints no keys, owner's request; they are in ⌘K and ⇧?, and each field announces its own); Tab also moves between fields and F2 edits the title. One Escape closes the pane and returns to the row. A click outside the pane takes the keys out of it (owner's request): a clicked row gets the cursor and its list the focus, while the pane stays open showing that row; anywhere else the list takes focus, and the rail, capture and search take it as usual. The rail, when active, holds real keyboard focus on its cursor.
- When clarifying, every next action must have a context (owner's rule). In Clarify, a next action without one shows its Context chip as "needed" in alert red, and ⌘↵ won't accept: it moves to that action and opens the context picker, which has no "No context" option and pre-selects nothing. Filing with V as a next action, into a project, or as a new project's first action asks for the context first; Esc files nothing. A new context can be typed there. Waiting For keeps its own rule (who or what you wait on); done-now, someday, reference and trash need no context.
- **K** is Clarify: you decide, one item at a time, on the Clarify screen; each item starts as "Your decision" and you set the project, context, dates, disposition or extra actions, then ⌘↵. Filing (V) is the quicker way for items whose place is obvious.
- A Weekly Review step is struck through only when you mark it with ⌘↵ "Reviewed" ("My head is empty" on the Mind sweep) and nothing in it is left open (owner's decision after the GTD critique: visiting is not reviewing, and a ⌘. walk-through strikes nothing). Finish lists every step not clear, with why ("5 open", "not marked reviewed"), and says "Your system is current" only when all are. Open counts: inbox empty, no stalled or flagged projects, no overdue next actions, no follow-ups due, no someday items due back, nothing in Upcoming already past its date.
- The Weekly Review's checks are built in (owner's decision). A project is **stalled** when it has no next or waiting action, or when nothing in it has been touched (edited or completed) for the stall threshold, 3 weeks by default and set in Settings › Weekly Review › Stalled after (type any whole number of weeks, 1–52). Every action records when it was last touched. The review also notes overdue next actions, follow-ups due, next or waiting items untouched for the threshold, and someday items due back. The stalled lamp and badge use the same rule everywhere (Projects, the rail's system check, the review). A project with a start date hasn't begun until that day is over (owner's decision, GTD's "a calendar entry is a next step"): before then it can't be stalled, whatever it holds, so a project waiting for a booked meeting gets the meeting's day as its start and is only flagged from the day after, if the meeting left no next action. Its idle clock runs from its start date (or creation, if later). It is still listed in every Weekly Review with its start date, so a start date can't quietly park it; on the day it starts, without a next action, it asks for one ("Starts today: add a next action").
- **Still current** (owner's decision after the review critique): in the review's Next actions, Waiting for and Projects steps, R on a row flagged as untouched for weeks (or the ticked rows) records "reviewed, still current": it counts as touching the item, so that flag clears and the stall clock restarts, and nothing else changes; a project is touched through its open actions. R clears only that flag: overdue, follow-up due and no next action are cleared by acting on them, and R on such a row says so. The key line shows R only while some row carries it. F2 rewrites an action or project in place ("Rewrite anything vague"), and in Get creative N on an area starts a new project filed in it, then asks for its first next action. D on Waiting for is the follow-up date and says so. The Finish tally counts only what happened while the review screen was open, never ordinary work done while a review stood open for days.
- The Weekly Review is resumable: leaving it to fix something (a list, Clarify, a project) and coming back returns to the same step with the same struck steps.
- Finish lists the steps still open (keys 1–6 jump to them), a tally of what the review did (clarified, done, added, completed) and records with ⌘↵. In the Projects step, N gives the focused project a next action directly.
- The "waiting on" prompt pre-selects nothing (unless the item already has a name, or you add inside a waiting-on group): Enter alone never picks someone by accident. The Move picker marks the item's current list and project.
- Overdue dates say so to screen readers, not only in red. Help, the palette and pickers are proper dialogs and comboboxes; a stopped Clarify is announced and focuses its first way forward.
- **Mind sweep** (GTD's "empty your head"): the Weekly Review's first step, and on its own from ⌘K › Mind sweep. A capture line beside a condensed incompletion trigger list (Work: projects started or to start, promises, calls and emails, meetings, waiting on others, documents, money, people, systems, learning; Home: promises, the home, errands, health, money, paperwork, occasions, hobbies, anything else). Each line is captured to the Inbox as it is entered, with no deciding yet; Get clear, next, is where it is clarified. "Captured in this review" lists exactly what the review's own capture lines (the Mind sweep's, and Get creative's) put in the Inbox, remembered by the review itself, never whatever else was captured while a review stood open (owner's bug report).
- **Look back** (GTD's "review the previous calendar"): a review step before Upcoming listing everything finished in the last two weeks, actions and projects, newest first, where follow-ups hide. T adds a follow-up next action to the item's project (or, loose, from anywhere), W a follow-up waiting for; nothing there is edited or marked done.
- **What fits now** (GTD's engage step: where you are decides what can be done; owner's decision: it asks only that, not time or energy): on Next Actions, F asks "Where are you?": tick one or more contexts; each tick applies at once and the picker comes back on the row just ticked; Done closes it, Anywhere shows every next action. Actions for other contexts fold into a last group "Elsewhere"; chases, having no place of their own, always stay; done rows step aside. The heading's count follows the filter ("5 of 21 actions fit"), a line above the list names the place ("Fits @errands") with Change and Show all, and "What fits now" sits in the heading when it is off, so it is in reach on a phone. The filter lapses at the end of the day.
- **Agendas** (GTD's list per person; owner's request after a GTD gap review): a list after Waiting For, one group per person, alphabetical. Each gathers what you have to take up with them (next actions whose With names them, shown with their context, e.g. @agenda or @phone) and what they owe you (Waiting For on them, with since when and the follow-up date), so a meeting, call or email with someone starts from one view. People aren't stored separately: a person is a name the system already uses in With or Waiting on, matched without regard to case. T adds something to take up with the person under the cursor (what, then a context), W something you're waiting on from them, E marks done (taken up, or received), and the View menu (⌥V) narrows the list to one person's agenda. It takes ⌃5 (owner's request after the rail critique: without a number every row below it was one off), so Projects is ⌃6.
- **With** (who an action is for): a next action's field, beside Repeat in the detail pane (W, or ⌘K › Who it's for), picked from everyone the system knows or typed new; No one clears it. On Next Actions the name rides before the subject in small caps, as a chase names who.
- **Clarifying asks first** (owner's decision after the GTD critique): each Inbox item opens on "Is it actionable?" (Y yes, S Someday/Maybe, R Reference, Del Trash), not on a pre-filled action. Yes gives an empty next action to put into words ("What is the very next physical step?"), the capture beside it as the source, and accepting with it empty is refused.
- **Get creative** (the review's last step before Finish): each area with its active projects, noting any with none, and a capture line for anything new (projects, ideas, someday wishes), which goes to the Inbox.
- **Calendars (the hard landscape)** (owner's request: work in Outlook, private in iCloud): Settings › General lists every subscribed calendar with its colour and name, then "Add a calendar". Any read-only ICS or webcal link works: Outlook's Publish calendar (ICS link), iCloud's public calendar (webcal link), or others. Adding takes the link first (the server checks it is a calendar and counts its appointments), then a name (the calendar's own name as the suggestion), then a colour from the context palette. On a calendar's row Enter replaces the link, F2 renames, C recolours and Delete removes it. The links stay on the server (the browser learns only names, colours and hosts); each calendar's last good copy is kept on the server's disk and in the browser (owner's request: the calendar was empty on every opening, and emptied when a click moved it into a month not yet fetched), so the Calendar, Look back and Upcoming show at once, from what is known, and change only when a fresher copy arrives; a copy older than 10 minutes is read again in the background, never while you wait (only a calendar never read before is waited for), a failed read keeps the last good copy and says so on its row, signing out clears the browser's copy, repeats (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly) are expanded with cancelled and moved occurrences, and Outlook's Windows time zones are converted to the owner's time zone as their browser reports it, never the server's (owner's bug report: on a UTC server a 12:15 lunch showed at 10:15). Times without a zone of their own are the owner's local time. Appointments can be linked to a project (owner's request): P on an appointment (in its details pane, on the Calendar, or in the review's Upcoming and Look back) picks the project, creates one, or unlinks it with No project; ⌘Z undoes. The link keeps the appointment's title, day and time. An upcoming linked appointment (today or later) is the project's next step: a project with it and no next action shows as scheduled (the grey clock), not stalled, and its lamp, its Projects row and its pane name the appointment ("Next step: Workshop with Nordplast, Mon 5 Oct 09:30. Add a next action when it has happened."). Once it has passed, the project needs a next action again. The project's pane lists its appointments above its actions, the passed ones faded; the review's Upcoming names the project after the time. J on a linked appointment (in the Calendar or its details pane) jumps to its project; J on that project jumps back to the appointment in the Calendar, as J already goes between an action and its project (owner's request). J on a project without an open action goes to its next linked appointment instead. Links follow their appointments (owner's decision): after every good read of a calendar (on opening a view, or Sync now), each upcoming link is checked against it. A changed title or time is taken over; an appointment moved to another day (a one-off, or one occurrence of a repeating meeting) takes its link with it; one that is gone (deleted, cancelled, or declined) is simply unlinked, so its project needs a next action again. Each change is said once in a toast. Links whose day has passed are left alone (a feed may drop old history), and a calendar that couldn't be read changes nothing. Meetings the owner declined are left out (owner's bug report: Outlook and iCloud can both keep a declined invitation in the published calendar). The feed doesn't name the owner, so the owner is the address on the most meetings in that calendar (an Apple ID's @icloud.com, @me.com and @mac.com count as one); a decline counts only from an attendee who isn't the meeting's organiser. Sync now (owner's request): the Calendar's sync button, ⌥S in the Calendar, or ⌘K › Sync calendars reads every calendar again at once, whatever the cache, and says so ("2 calendars synced", or which couldn't be read, their last copy kept); the button's tooltip gives the time of the last sync. Appointments show read-only, in their calendar's colour, in the Calendar (never dragged or opened; a legend hides a calendar for a while in this browser), in Look back and in Upcoming ("Work · 09:30–10:00"). A single Outlook link set before becomes the first calendar, named Outlook.
- On the review's Someday step, A activates an item (E is Done everywhere).
- **Week view with hours** (owner's request): on a desktop the Calendar's week is a regular calendar week that fits the window without scrolling, showing the hours set in Settings › General › Hours in the week (07:00–19:00 by default; a week with an appointment outside them stretches to show it), with no more than three all-day rows a day before "+N more": what has no time of day (deadlines, starts, follow-ups, projects, all-day appointments) sits in a band across the top; timed appointments from the subscribed calendars are placed at their hours, overlapping ones side by side, with a line at the current time on today. Actions and projects stay day-based (they have no time of day).
- **Appointment details** (owner's request): clicking an appointment (a Calendar bar or hour block, a phone agenda row, a Look back or Upcoming row) or Enter on it opens its details, read-only: title and calendar, when, where, a Join button for a Teams, Zoom, Meet or Webex link, the organizer, attendees grouped by answer (accepted, tentative, no answer yet, declined; optional marked) with a count line, and the description with its links. Only what the calendar's feed shares can be shown: published and shared calendars usually leave attendees (and, in Outlook without "All details", the description) out, and the pane says so. T and W add a next action or waiting for, as in every pane.
- **Checklists** (owner's request; GTD's checklists, which Allen keeps with Reference as support material): reusable lists such as packing for a trip, closing the month or a new starter's first week, kept to be run when they are relevant. One kind serves both uses: a checklist can be ticked through or simply read. It is a rail stop of its own after Reference (⌃⇧3; Done moved to ⌃⇧4 and Trash to ⌃⇧5, owner's decision). The view has two levels with their own addresses (#checklists, #checklists/<id>): every checklist (grouped by area once any has one; name, items, how far this run has got, last finished), and one checklist's items, opened with Enter and left with Esc. Ticking only ticks (owner's decision): E ticks or unticks and moves the cursor on to the next open item; ticks are the run's progress and stay until Start over (⌥V, ⌘K, or the heading's "Start over"), which clears them, ⌘Z bringing them back. Ticking the last item records when the run was finished. Items keep their place when ticked (greyed and struck through, like done actions); N adds an item below the cursor, and Enter after typing one starts the next, so a list is typed in one go; L turns a row into a section heading (Label Caps) or back; F2 or Enter rewrites; ⌥↑↓ reorders; Delete removes. T makes a next action from the item under the cursor (GTD: a checklist is a trigger for new actions), leaving the item as it is. A on either level sets the checklist's area. Deleted checklists go to the Trash with their items. On a phone a tap ticks and the More sheet lists Checklists after Reference.
- The Weekly Review's **Checklists** step comes after Upcoming (GTD's "get current" ends with "review any relevant checklists"): every checklist with its area, how far a run has got and when it was last finished; Enter opens one (the review keeps its place), T adds a next action. Nothing in it counts as open. On Finish, 1–9 and 0 jump back to the ten steps.
- An Inbox item can be filed as a checklist (V › Checklist, beside Reference; or in Clarify, File as › Whole item → New checklist): its first line names the checklist and its other lines become the items (bullets, numbers and boxes dropped; a line ending in a colon, or a Markdown heading, becomes a section heading). Clarify shows the items it will get. An item with files can't become a checklist (a checklist keeps no files), and says to file it as Reference instead.
- **Habits are repeating checklists** (owner's request and decision; GTD covers routines with checklists run daily or weekly, not a separate tracker): R on a checklist (or ⌥V, ⌘K) sets it to repeat every day or every week, or not at all. Each item is then a habit. Its ticks clear by themselves when the day or week turns (weeks start on the day set in Settings › Calendar), and every tick is kept as a record of the day it was done, so there is no Start over. Unticking removes this period's record; ⌘Z restores it; removing a habit removes its record with it. The heading counts the period ("3 of 5 today", "All 5 done this week"), and finishing them all records the run. Each habit row carries its last four weeks as a quiet strip (28 days, or 8 weeks; the last two weeks on a phone), filled where it was done, the current day or week outlined, each square naming its day on hover, and beside it the current run ("5 days", "3 weeks"; today not yet done doesn't break it). Every checklist shows "Repeats" ("Every day"). In the Weekly Review's Checklists step a routine says how its week went ("Every day · all done 5 of the last 7 days"), to ask whether each habit still serves. The export lists each habit with how many of the last 28 days it was done. Not included (owner's decision): weekday-only habits, "N times a week", and any rail signal.
- Search finds checklists by name or by any of their items (the hit names the item: "Checklists · Passport"); Enter opens the checklist. The Weekly Review remembers its step by name, so adding a step never moves a review in progress onto another one.
