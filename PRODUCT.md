# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Delegated: a TypeScript web app served locally. React + Vite on the front end, a small Node server (Hono) for file parsing, calendar feeds and the login, and SQLite for storage. Chosen because it keeps everything on one machine, keeps calendar links and secrets off the client, and makes dense editable lists straightforward. It runs in the browser at localhost; a desktop wrapper can come later without a rewrite.

## Users

One person: the owner, on their own Mac. They practice (or want to practice) David Allen's Getting Things Done and want the capture → clarify → organize loop to take less effort. The expected power use is keyboard-heavy and list-centric, in the spirit of the classic Outlook Tasks view.

## Product Purpose

A personal GTD system where capture has no friction and clarification is quick. The owner dumps "stuff" into an inbox by typing or uploading documents, then clarifies it one item at a time, deciding for each whether it is actionable and what it becomes: projects, next actions, waiting-for, someday/maybe, reference, or trash. The result lives in compact, directly editable lists.

Success means the inbox reaches zero regularly and every active project has a next action. The owner trusts the lists enough to work from them.

## Positioning

Clarifying is the GTD step people skip because it takes thought. This product makes that thinking fast: one item at a time, GTD's questions asked in order, every decision a keystroke. Nothing moves out of the inbox without the owner's say-so. There are no AI features (owner's decision): the owner does the thinking, and the app keeps it quick.

## Operating Context

- Runs locally in a browser tab (no login on localhost), or self-hosted on the owner's VPS at gtd.rikard.me behind HTTPS with a single-owner login: password + TOTP authenticator code, one-time recovery codes, 30-day HttpOnly sessions, and login rate limiting. There is one user and no sync between instances. Deployment is documented in DEPLOY.md.
- Capture comes from free typing (quick-add) and from uploaded documents (the types are listed under Capture inputs below).
- Includes a recurring Weekly Review ritual, with its checks built in.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- GTD lists at launch: Inbox, Projects, Next Actions, Contexts (tags on next actions, e.g. @computer, @phone), Waiting For, Someday/Maybe, Reference, Checklists, Weekly Review.
- The clarify step is the owner's decision, one item at a time.
- Project and next-action lists are compact, inline-editable tables/lists (the Outlook Tasks model).
- Next Actions are grouped by context by default (where the owner is and what tools are at hand); grouping by project is also offered (owner's request).
- Waiting For is grouped by who or what it waits on (Group by waiting on), never by project; the Waiting on column is hidden while grouped by it. A new item added inside a group offers that name first. The names offered when asking "waiting on" are the ones on items in Waiting For right now; an item moved back to Next or Someday drops its waiting on, since and follow-up (⌘Z brings them back).
- There is no importance marker (owner's decision after the second GTD critique, retiring the Outlook-style Important mark, its ⌘I/Ins keys and the Importance grouping): GTD leaves priority to the judgement of the moment, made against the horizons (Horizons, goals) after context, time and energy, rather than coded on actions. Existing marks are kept in the data but no longer shown.
- **Keyboard-only is a hard requirement.** The whole interface must work without a mouse. Every view, list, row, field, action, dialog, and Clarify decision needs a keyboard shortcut or a keyboard path. The mouse is optional and never required.
- Shortcuts follow **Outlook on the web** conventions, not classic desktop Outlook. On the Mac, Ctrl becomes ⌘ everywhere (owner's rule). The one exception is the go-to keys: Control on every system, ⌃ on the Mac too (owner's decision), in rail order counted per group so every key sits under one hand: ⌃1–6 for the first group (Inbox, Calendar, Next Actions, Waiting For, Agendas, Projects) and ⌃⇧1–5 for the second group and the rail's foot (Someday, Reference, Checklists, then Done and Trash, which sit together at the foot as places to look back at, after the rail critique; owner's request: ⌃7–9 needed a second hand), because the browser owns ⌘1–8 (tabs), macOS owns ⌘⇧3–5 (screenshots) and ⌘⌥ is too awkward to hold. On Windows and Linux the page takes Ctrl+1–6 over the browser's tab switching; Ctrl+Shift+digits are free. Control with a letter is never used for them: on the Mac those edit text (⌃A, ⌃E), elsewhere the browser owns many (Ctrl+W, Ctrl+R). Mac aliases cover missing keys (Home/End). Browser- and OS-reserved keys are never used. GTD-only actions get extra single-letter keys in the same style. Capture is in-app only for now; a global hotkey is deferred. There are no confirmation dialogs; every action is undoable with ⌘Z, announced in a brief toast (there is no status bar). Shortcuts are discovered through the ⌘K command palette, not printed on the lists. It follows focus (owner's decision after the keys critique, which folded the ⇧? cheat sheet into it and then retired ⇧?): recently used commands first, then what can be done to the row, item or details pane under the cursor, headed by its name ("“Launch the site”"; one group, its fields last), then the rest of the place you are in (the list's own commands, its View, moving around and selecting), then the app-wide commands, quieter, under Everywhere; every command shows every key it answers to, and typing searches every command, nearest first. A command says what it does, verb first (owner's rule): a field is set ("Set due date", "Set context", never the field's bare name), the cursor goes ("Go to the next row"), a view is shown ("Show the week"), a menu is opened ("Open the View menu: group and sort"); "New project" keeps the File › New form. And it is short: the action alone, no explanation in brackets and nothing the place already says ("Add a next action", not "Add a next action (to this row's project)"; J on a project names the one place it will go, "Jump to its next action" or "Jump back to the reference"). The exceptions are the rail, where every stop shows its shortcut as a key cap, and the letter-key modes, Clarify and the Weekly Review: each carries one quiet line naming its few keys (owner's decisions after the first and fourth critiques). Key legends show modifier symbols on every system (⌃ Control, ⇧ Shift; ⌘ and ⌥ on the Mac, ◇ for Alt elsewhere), never "Ctrl+Shift+" words (owner's decision).
- Target browsers: Firefox and Microsoft Edge (Chromium) on macOS. Both must be tested, including every shortcut.
- Data is local-first in SQLite.
- Terminology follows GTD: stuff, capture, clarify, organize, reflect, engage, next action, project, context, waiting for, someday/maybe, reference.
- Naming: next actions and projects are both written verb-first ("Call Anna about the Q3 figures", "Update the user guide for the new software"). The project title names the outcome; "Done looks like" (natural planning, below) says what will be true when it's done.
- **Capture inputs:** typed text, pasted text or email, and uploaded PDF, DOCX, TXT/MD, images, .eml and .msg. A document enters as one inbox item; clarifying may turn it into several outcomes, and the source file stays linked.
- **Done items:** leave the active lists but stay on their project's timeline and in a searchable, dated Done log. The Weekly Review uses this log.
- Completing a project (E or its done box on Projects, E in the Calendar or the Weekly Review) marks its open next actions and waiting fors done with it; they stay struck through on their lists until archived, like any done action (owner's request); the toast says how many ("“Launch the site” complete · 3 open actions done with it", owner's request). Not done on the project brings them back to their lists. Someday actions in it stay as they are.
- **Project timeline** (owner's request): a project's detail pane lists every action it has had, in the order they were created (oldest first, the newest just above "Add a next action"): next actions, Waiting For items and someday ones, and done ones struck through, including those archived to Done. Deleted actions don't show. Each row ends with its date: when it was done, or when it was added; the heading counts the open ones and notes how many are done.
- **Recurring actions:** the next occurrence is created when the current one is marked done, with its dates moved forward. Recurrence is set as natural text ("every 3 months", "every mon").
- **Waiting For:** every item must name who or what it is waiting on. Every way into Waiting For asks for it first (moving, delegating, creating directly, filing from the Inbox, Clarify), and cancelling changes nothing. Items also record since when (set automatically), a follow-up date that resurfaces the item as something to chase, and an optional project link. A waiting item counts as its project's next step, so the project isn't flagged as stalled.
- **Tickler:** a someday action or someday project can get a "bring back on" date (owner's decision: bring back defers a decision, and anything on Next Actions, Waiting For or Projects is already decided, so it never goes back to the Inbox; a start date is how a decided item waits for its day). Making an item current drops its date (⌘Z restores it). On that date it comes back for a fresh decision, as GTD's tickler does, without losing anything (owner's decision after the GTD critique; the first version copied its text to the Inbox and trashed the original, losing its project, context, who it waited on and its dates): the item stays exactly where and what it was, and the Inbox gets an entry pointing at it, "Due back: Call Anna". Clarifying that entry asks "It's back. Is it still right?", naming where it lives, with the choices Enter keep it as it is, A make it current (a someday action becomes a next action, asking its context if it has none; a someday project becomes active), E done (a project with its open actions), B bring it back again later, J go to it, Delete drop it (to the Trash). V on the entry offers the same choices; E and Delete in the Inbox do what they say to the item. Each is one ⌘Z. The review's Someday step flags anything whose entry still waits. Defer (start) dates hide next actions until their date.
- **Areas of focus:** projects belong to areas (#Work, #Home, #Health…; shown with a # as contexts are with an @, owner's decision), which group the Projects list and the review. There is no Areas page (owner's decision: grouping Projects by area already shows it); areas are added from any area picker and renamed, reordered or deleted in Settings › Areas. Projects can be narrowed to areas (owner's request): F on Projects asks "Which areas?" (No area included): picking an area narrows the list to it and closes the picker (owner's request); ⇧↵, or a ⇧- or ⌘-click, adds an area to those shown (or takes one off) and keeps the picker open for the next; Every area clears it. It stays until cleared (a way of looking, not a moment, unlike What fits now). The heading's count follows it ("4 of 12 active projects"), a line above the list names the areas with Change and Show all, the View menu (⌥V) offers it too, and "Filter by area" sits in the heading when it is off. A new project made while narrowed takes the first chosen area, so it stays in sight.
- **Reference** is Allen's general-reference file: one A–Z list, no folders or topics to keep up. Retrieval is by title, by search and by project (Reference critique): / on the list opens "Find a reference", where typing a title narrows the list (titles that start with the words first, the project beside each) and Enter puts the cursor on it (the list's letters are its commands, so the typing happens in a picker); ⌥Q search matches a reference's notes, the project it supports, its files' names and the text read out of them (a PDF's words, an email's body), and says which project a hit supports. A filed PDF or text file shows its excerpt in the pane, as it did in the Inbox; Enter on a file under the pane's cursor opens it. Filing from Clarify keeps the captured words the way V › Reference does: your notes, then the capture without its title line (unless you retitled it). The export's reference.md lists each reference A–Z with its project and its files' names.
- **A reference is a note, a list or a document** (owner's request; Allen's reference file holds anything you might look up, so all three stay in the one A–Z list). A mark before each title tells them apart: a note (its first line follows in grey), a list ("12 items"), a document (a reference with a file and no notes: "Lease.pdf · PDF"). N makes a note; ⌘K › New list makes a list, which opens to be filled once it is named. ⌘K › Show as a list (or Show as a note), on the list or in the pane, switches one: a note's lines become items, without their bullets, numbers or boxes, a heading or short line ending in a colon becoming a section heading; back again, the items read as lines. A list is kept as its notes, one item a line, "## " marking a section heading, so search, the export (as a Markdown list), ⌘Z and the lock work on it as on any note. Nothing on a list can be ticked: it is reference, not action (a list to run is a checklist). Enter on a list opens it across the width, as a checklist opens ("Reference › Kids' sizes", its own address, Esc back up): N adds below (Enter after each starts the next), L makes a row a section heading, F2 or Enter rewrites, Delete removes, ⌥↑↓ or a drag reorders, a pasted list becomes items. In the pane a list shows as rows: a click or Enter rewrites one (emptied, it goes), N adds at the end, Open the list goes to the whole list. A locked list opens with the lock password, like any locked reference.
- **Documents are read in the app** (owner's request: never downloaded just to be read). A file opens in the viewer, over the list, with the details pane beside it: a click on a file in any details pane (Enter under the pane's cursor), or Enter on a document reference. PDFs show in the browser's own viewer, images as they are; Word (.docx), email (.eml, .msg), CSV and text (and Markdown, JSON, HTML) as a page the server makes, in the app's light or dark, an email's date in the owner's time zone. Every page is shown in a sandboxed frame under the strictest policy: nothing in a document can run, fetch or reach the app. ← → step through the item's files, Esc or Space closes, the bar downloads it or opens it in a tab of its own (⌘-click on a file still opens a tab). Excel and PowerPoint aren't shown yet: they download (owner's choice of order; showing them needs a converter on the server). A locked file is decrypted in the browser: a PDF, image, CSV or text shows as usual; a locked Word or email file only downloads, since showing it would have the server read it.
- **The phone is slimmer** (owner's request): below 820px wide the app is the Inbox, the Calendar and the lists (Next Actions, Waiting For, Agendas, Projects, Checklists, Reference, Someday, Done and Trash), from a bottom bar: Inbox, Calendar, Next, Waiting, Lists. The Weekly Review, Settings and Horizons are desktop work. Lists are easier to work with on a phone: two-line rows (the whole title, and what the columns hold as a line under it), the swipes (right Done, left Trash), a tap for details, and a + button where a thumb is, doing what N does. A tablet held landscape and the desktop keep everything.
- **Locked references** (owner's request: some reference is secret, such as codes and account details): L on Reference (or ⌘K › Lock with password in its pane) locks a reference with one lock password, set the first time and changed in Settings › Data › Lock password (owner's decision: one shared password, not one per item). Its notes and files are encrypted in the browser (AES-GCM, with a random key that the server keeps only wrapped by a key derived from the password), so the server, its disk, its backups and the exports hold only ciphertext; the title and project stay readable, so the list, search and J still find it (owner's decision). Its file names are encrypted too, and nothing is read out of its files for search. On the list a small lock replaces the notes' first line. Its pane, while shut, says what is locked and takes the password right there; unlocked, every locked reference reads and edits as usual (new files are encrypted on the way up) until 5 minutes pass without a key or a click, Lock now (⌘K, or the pane's line), or the tab closes or reloads (owner's decision). Remove the lock (L again) puts notes and files back in the clear. A forgotten password can't be reset by anyone: what it locks is lost. Locking and removing the lock aren't undone with ⌘Z. It needs https (or localhost), where browsers allow the encryption.
- **Project support:** each project has a notes page and attached files, and its **support material** (owner's request; GTD keeps project support material apart from the actions): references and checklists linked to it. P on a reference or a checklist (in its list, its pane, or inside the checklist) sets the project it supports, the same picker everywhere with the current one marked, which can also start a new project. The project's pane lists them under "Support material", after its actions and before the notes, references first, A–Z: a reference opens in the pane (Esc comes back), and so does a checklist (owner's request), slimmed to running it: its items to tick (Enter or a click; a routine ticks today, each habit with its run), section headings as quiet labels, a line to add an item at the end (N), Start over, J to the project, and Open in Checklists for building it (sections, order, a routine's four weeks and earlier days). The section shows only when something is linked; ⌘K › Link support material links one from the pane; one that supports another project moves over, and the picker says which. Checklists show a Project column once any supports one. J on a reference (in Reference or its pane) or a checklist (in the list of checklists, or inside one) jumps to the project it supports, and J on that project comes back to it while it is still linked, as J already goes between an action or an appointment and its project (owner's request); without a project it says P links one.
- **Weekly Review:** a guided checklist in David Allen's order and his three phases (owner's decision after the GTD critique, 1 October): **Get clear**: mind sweep → Inbox to zero (collect, then process once: owner's decision, since here the sweep captures into the Inbox, so sweeping after zero would only refill it; there is no loose-papers step, owner's decision); **Get current**: next actions → look back (the previous calendar) → upcoming (the calendar ahead) → Waiting For → projects → checklists; **Get creative**: Someday/Maybe → get creative. The strip names each phase where it begins. What the sweep captures is clarified in Inbox to zero right after; anything Get creative captures later reopens it, and Finish asks for it to be clarified. Built-in checks flag stalled projects, stale waiting items and overdue actions. The date of the last review is shown.
- **Export:** everything can be exported as Markdown (one file per list) plus a JSON dump, so there is no lock-in. Automatic backup and import are not in scope for now.
- **Theme:** follows the macOS appearance, with a light daytime theme and a dark evening theme, both designed in their own right.
- **Not in scope yet:** global capture hotkey, multiple devices, import.
- No clarification writes "Captured: …" into an action's notes (owner's decision). Clarifying carries the item's own notes (everything under its first line) over to the first action as they are; a one-line capture leaves the notes empty.
- **Calendar** (owner's request): GTD's hard landscape, second in the rail right after the Inbox (⌃2), because it is checked before the lists are worked. Week, month and year views of the dates the system holds: an action's start (defer) and due, a project's start and due (projects gained a start date for it), Waiting For follow-ups and Someday ticklers. Start and due draw one bar across the days between; either end drags to change that date, the bar drags to move both. Done and deleted work stays off it. Delete (or Backspace) on an action or project under the Calendar's cursor sends it to the Trash, a project with its open actions as on Projects, ⌘Z bringing it back, and the cursor moves on to the day's next item (owner's request); appointments from the subscribed calendars can't be deleted here, they belong to their calendar. Marking done works the same way (owner's request): E on the item under the cursor, or, in the day view and the phone's week agenda, the lists' Complete box at the start of each action and project row. The item is struck through, then leaves the calendar (done work stays off it), a project closing its open actions with it as on Projects; ⌘Z brings it back. Appointments have no box. In the Calendar T and W add, as everywhere (letters mean one thing): T (or N) a day-specific action on the day under the cursor (start and due that day, so it stays off Next Actions until then), W a waiting for whose follow-up is that day; Home (or Outlook's ⌥⇧Y) goes to today. Weeks start on Monday, or Sunday in Settings › Calendar.
- On Projects and in the Weekly Review's Projects step, W adds a Waiting For item straight to the project under the cursor (what, then who or what; waiting since today), as T adds a next action (owner's request: the two are a pair, both single letters, in a project's detail pane too; the Weekly Review, once a week, moved to ⇧R); a waiting item counts as the project's next step.
- A Waiting For item can be added from anywhere with ⌥W (owner's request): what you're waiting for, who or what you wait on (required, as everywhere in Waiting For; recent names are offered), then its project if any. It is waiting since today; a follow-up date is set on the item.
- A next action can be added from anywhere with ⌥T (owner's request): what to do, its context (required, as everywhere), then its project if it has one ("No project" first; a new name creates the project). It lands on Next Actions and the view stays where it was.
- A project can be started from anywhere with ⌥N (owner's request): its outcome (verb first), its area or none, then its first next action with a context, as GTD asks of every project; Esc at that step keeps the project without one. On the Projects list N still adds one in place.
- When clarifying by hand, an item can be made a project directly (⇧P, or File as › Whole item → New project; owner's request): the project takes the item's name, the proposed actions go into it, and the first action is ready to be named as the next step.
- A next action that turns out to need more than one step can be turned into a project (⇧P, the Move picker, ⌘K; owner's request): the action becomes the project's outcome and carries its notes, files, start/due dates, tickler and area (from the project it was in, whose name is noted); the first next action is asked for at once, keeping the old action's context.
- Deleted items are kept for a week by default in the Trash (⌃⇧5), adjustable in Settings (1–365 days), and can be restored where they were until then; after that the server removes them and their files (owner's request).
- Done works as in classic Outlook (owner's decision): a done action stays on its list, greyed and struck through at the bottom of its group, until archived; each action list can show or hide its done actions and archive them to Done: ⇧E archives every done item on every list at once (owner's request), and a list's View menu archives that list alone. A completed project is the exception: it goes straight to Done, the only place completed projects are shown (owner's request). Done holds everything archived, actions and completed projects alike (owner's request: like the Trash, it shows everything), grouped by the day it was done or by project; E puts an item back (a project with the actions closed along with it) and Delete trashes it. Existing done actions were treated as already archived.
- Each view has its own address (#inbox, #projects, #reference…), so the browser's Back and Forward move between views and a reload or bookmark lands on the same list (owner's request). Clarify has an entry (Back leaves it) but can't be reopened from an address, so it lands on the Inbox; search is a query, not a place, and adds no entry. The tab title names the view ("Projects · Stiltje").

## Product Principles

1. **Capture must be instant.** Getting something into the inbox should never require deciding anything.
2. **The owner decides.** Nothing is filed, changed or learned without an explicit decision; there are no AI features.
3. **Density over decoration.** The lists are working surfaces: many rows visible and editable in place.
4. **Keyboard first, everything has a shortcut.** If an action can't be done from the keyboard, it isn't finished.
5. **Faithful to GTD.** Use the method's vocabulary and flow rather than inventing a new productivity model.
6. **The owner's data stays with the owner.** It is local-first and portable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Full keyboard operability is a product requirement, not just an accessibility nicety. Focus must always be visible, and nothing can trap it. Every command must be discoverable from the keyboard (a shortcut reference and command palette). Semantic markup (grid/listbox roles) should keep the lists usable with a screen reader: the active list holds real keyboard focus and points `aria-activedescendant` at the cursor row, "selected" means ticked rows only (a plain move or click clears them, as in Finder and Explorer; ⇧ extends a range, ⌘/Ctrl-click ticks one, and a drag draws a selection rectangle, adding to the selection with ⌘/Ctrl), and every view change lands focus on the new list.
- The Weekly Review's progress survives closing the tab (a review untouched for a week starts over). Finish leads with what was cleared ("4 of 6 steps clear · 3 items clarified"), then lists what is still open. It never claims more than is true: the tape reads "Ready to record" while steps are open, and the recorded message only says "Your system is current" when none are (otherwise it says how many were left open). Get clear lists the Inbox as a list like the other steps (kind, subject, files, captured); V files the focused or ticked items, E marks them done (two-minute rule) and Delete trashes them, all without leaving the review. K runs Clarify inside the step, under the review's step bar; Esc returns to the list (owner's decision: clarifying and filing never take the owner out of the review). The Projects step shows each project's next action, or who it waits on. The rail says "not yet" before a first review and only turns red once a review is more than a week old.
- Toasts name the item when there is one ("“Pay the VAT for Q3” done"). ⌘Z returns the cursor to the restored row. A command on a group heading says so instead of doing nothing, and Esc from the capture bar says the draft is kept.
- Filing (V, "File", in the Inbox, or from an Inbox item's detail pane) covers the whole clarify decision in one picker, in GTD order: a list; done now (two-minute rule) or trash; an existing project; or a new project (type its name, as the placeholder says; the item becomes its first action). Move and File as list destinations in the same order with the same names. Filing something you will do (Next Actions, Waiting For, a project, or a new project) asks GTD's question first, one item at a time (owner's decision after the second GTD critique: V used to file the captured words as they were, many at once): "What's the very next physical step?" (or "What are you waiting for?"), with the item's own words offered to rewrite, then its context (or who it waits on); with several items ticked it goes through them in turn ("2 of 5"), each its own ⌘Z, and Esc stops there, leaving the rest in the Inbox. Someday/Maybe, Reference, Checklist, Done and Trash stay one step.
- The rail is a status panel: the Inbox (an ordinary rail item with its count), the lists in order of use with counts only where they ask for something (overdue on Next Actions; to chase; stalled; done today), and a system check (review age, oldest Inbox item; stalled projects and follow-ups due show on the Projects and Waiting For stops instead) whose rows open where each is fixed. On phones it becomes a bottom tab bar (Inbox, Next, Waiting, More).
- The detail pane opens with focus on the pane, not in a field. It is walked like every other region (owner's decision): a cursor sits on one field at a time, starting on the item's title; ↑↓ move it through the fields, the timeline's rows and the add line; Enter opens the field's picker or edits it. Each field's key still jumps straight to it and opens it, taking the cursor along (the pane prints no keys, owner's request; they are in ⌘K, and each field announces its own); Tab also moves between fields and F2 edits the title. One Escape closes the pane and returns to the row. A click outside the pane takes the keys out of it (owner's request): a clicked row gets the cursor and its list the focus, while the pane stays open showing that row; anywhere else the list takes focus, and the rail, capture and search take it as usual. The rail, when active, holds real keyboard focus on its cursor.
- Clarify has no Skip (owner's decision after the GTD critique: GTD never puts anything back in the in-tray): every item gets a decision in turn, even if that is Someday or the tickler.
- When clarifying, every next action must have a context (owner's rule). In Clarify, a next action without one shows its Context chip as "needed" in alert red, and ⌘↵ won't accept: it moves to that action and opens the context picker, which has no "No context" option and pre-selects nothing. Filing with V as a next action, into a project, or as a new project's first action asks for the context first; Esc files nothing. A new context can be typed there. Waiting For keeps its own rule (who or what you wait on); done-now, someday, reference and trash need no context.
- **K** is Clarify: you decide, one item at a time, on the Clarify screen; each item starts as "Your decision" and you set the project, context, dates, disposition or extra actions, then ⌘↵. Filing (V) is the quicker way for items whose place is obvious.
